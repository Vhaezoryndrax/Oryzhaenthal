# Oryzhaenthal

> The compass pointed toward a footprint. The traveler had not yet lifted his foot.

## The Observatory of Pale Sand

Oryzhaenthal is a circular observatory built where the desert's dunes stop moving. Its roof is open, but its instruments face the floor. The observers claim that certain stars are visible only from the side of the sky that has been buried.

At the center stands a brass compass upon a column of pale sand. The column does not collapse. The compass needle turns slowly, pausing whenever a visitor remembers a journey that ended somewhere other than intended.

Around the column lie twelve stone footprints. None matches a living foot. Each points toward the center, though observers returning at dawn insist that one has spent the night facing outward.

## The Cartographer with the Folded Wing

A cartographer arrived carrying a map wrapped around a long white bone. Beneath his cloak, one shoulder rose higher than the other, and a folded wing could be heard scraping against the stone doorway.

He unrolled the map before the compass. It showed no roads, mountains, or villages. It showed twelve footprints arranged around a thirteenth, which was smaller and drawn in a color the observers could see only from the corner of their eyes.

The compass stopped turning. The cartographer asked which traveler had remained at the center. Nobody admitted recognizing the thirteenth footprint.

He left the map upon the sand column. During the night, a narrow wing-shaped shadow crossed its surface. By morning, the white bone had become hollow enough to carry the sound of distant steps.

## A Calculation of Untraveled Distance

The observers attempted to measure the interval between the footprints. Their instruments agreed until they reached the smallest mark upon the map.

There, every measurement described a room containing a sleeping person and an open window. Some rooms belonged to the observers themselves. Others belonged to strangers whose names appeared as numbers upon the instruments.

One observer found his childhood bedroom inside a calculation. The window faced the desert, although the original room had overlooked a river. Beneath the bed lay a brass compass whose needle pointed toward someone standing outside the window.

He erased the calculation. The corresponding footprint vanished from the observatory's floor, leaving a shallow depression filled with pale sand. In that depression, a small star remained visible after sunrise.

## The Creature beneath the Column

The column's sand sometimes becomes transparent. Through it, observers see a vast scaled creature coiled beneath the instruments. Its body circles a dark opening in which the stars appear much closer than they should.

The creature's wings are folded across its eyes. Its claws hold the ends of a narrow road, stretching it between them like a length of thread. Travelers upon that road can be seen walking toward a doorway shaped like the observatory's entrance.

An observer lowered a measuring rod into the sand. The creature moved one claw, and the rod emerged from the floor beside him, bearing marks of distances he had not yet traveled.

He set it against the wall. Its shadow remained inside the column, turning slowly around the dark opening. The compass resumed its motion only after he stopped trying to retrieve that shadow.

## The Sequence upon the White Bone

**Ory — zhaen — thal — uvek.**

These sounds are heard when the hollow bone is held toward the floor. The observers record them without punctuation, because every pause seems to change the direction of a footprint.

The first sound resembles a greeting spoken from far away. The second resembles a name heard through sleep. The third cannot be distinguished from the movement of sand beneath the column. The last leaves a taste of rain upon the listener's tongue.

A visitor spoke the sequence beneath the open roof. The stars above remained still. The star in the shallow depression brightened until its light showed a folded wing beneath the visitor's cloak.

He covered the depression with the map. The thirteenth footprint acquired a shadow that continued walking after he had left.

## Questions from the Buried Sky

> Where does the compass point when the needle stops?
>
> Toward the foot that has not chosen its traveler.
>
> Who holds the road beneath the column?
>
> The one whose sleep keeps the stars from falling.
>
> Why does the smallest footprint remain upon the map?
>
> Because the doorway has not decided which side is the desert.

The observers heard these replies through the white bone. Their records disagree about the speaker's voice but agree that the final answer ended with the sound of a window being closed.

The next morning, every instrument showed a room without a window. The person sleeping inside each room remained unchanged.

## The Footstep before Arrival

The observatory continues measuring distances beneath the sky. Its observers leave the hollow bone near the column and keep the map unrolled, though they avoid looking directly at its smallest mark.

The compass pauses more often in winter. Whenever it does, one footprint fills briefly with water that reflects a doorway beneath a folded wing.

Oryzhaenthal remains where the dunes refuse to move. Under its floor, a sleeping creature holds a road between two claws, waiting for the traveler whose first step has already reached the other end.
